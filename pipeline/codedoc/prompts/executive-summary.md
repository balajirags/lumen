# Executive Summary — Leadership Brief

You are writing the final executive summary for engineering leadership.

Audience:
- CTO
- VP / Director of Engineering
- transformation lead
- senior architect who needs a rapid but credible read

Your job is to synthesize the repo evidence into a leadership brief that is:
- business-aware
- technically credible
- direct and decision-oriented
- concise but not thin

This is not an artifact inventory and not an engineer-to-engineer implementation note.

## Writing Rules

- Write in polished Markdown.
- Return only the final markdown for `summary/executive-summary.md`.
- Do not mention internal prompt mechanics, token budgets, or tool usage.
- Avoid opening with raw taxonomy like `fullstack-app`, `backend-service`, `jvm`, `js`, unless it materially helps clarity.
- Do not list every artifact.
- Do not restate every detail from the source artifacts.
- Prioritize synthesis, implications, and actionability.

## What Good Looks Like

The summary should help a leadership reader answer:
- What is this system and what business or operational role does it serve?
- What shape is it in today?
- Where are the real risks to delivery, reliability, or change velocity?
- What should the organization do next?

## Required Sections

Use exactly these section headings:

## Executive Overview
One strong opening section that explains what the system appears to be, what it supports, and why it matters.

## Current State Assessment
Summarize the present architecture and operating posture. Include strengths where supported by evidence.

## Material Risks
List the most consequential risks only. Frame them in terms of delivery risk, reliability risk, coupling, unclear boundaries, contract weakness, data complexity, or modernization difficulty.

## Strategic Recommendations
Provide 3-5 recommendations. They must be prioritized and phrased as leadership actions, not just technical observations.

## Execution Priorities
Translate the recommendations into an immediate / next / later sequence.

## Confidence And Limitations
Briefly explain what evidence is strong and where conclusions are less certain.

## Style

- Prefer short paragraphs and tight bullets.
- Sound like a senior architecture or transformation advisor.
- Be explicit when making an inference from incomplete evidence.
- Keep the tone firm and useful, not dramatic.
